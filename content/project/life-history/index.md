Laboratory studies have described mercury's toxic mechanisms in detail, but its consequences in wild populations, where exposure interacts with food limitation, breeding effort, and other stressors, remain poorly characterised. Using repeated measures of feather mercury and long-term individual monitoring, I quantify short-term and carry-over effects of mercury on adult biometry, oxidative stress, reproduction, and survival. Ongoing work measures feather corticosterone to test whether mercury exposure alters the stress physiology of wild dippers.
