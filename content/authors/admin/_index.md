I am a PhD candidate in ecotoxicology and ecophysiology at the [Swiss Ornithological Institute](https://www.vogelwarte.ch/en/) (Vogelwarte) and the [University of Lausanne](https://www.unil.ch/dee/en/home.html). I investigate the sources of, and individual variation in, mercury and metal exposure in wild birds, and its consequences for individuals, from mitochondrial function and oxidative stress to growth, reproduction, and survival.

Most of my work uses a long-term, individual-based population of white-throated dippers (*Cinclus cinclus*) in the Chartreuse mountains, France, run with Dr Blandine Doligez (CNRS Lyon). I combine fieldwork, lab assays of mitochondrial function (plate-reader respirometry), oxidative stress, and corticosterone, and Bayesian statistics (mixed models, quantitative genetics, and phylogenetic meta-analysis). My PhD is supervised by Dr Pierre Bize and Prof. Philippe Christe, and funded by the Swiss National Science Foundation ([SNSF grant 212971](https://data.snf.ch/grants/grant/212971)).

My interest in contaminants and wild birds started during my Bachelor's at the University of Aberdeen, after time spent volunteering at a wildlife rehabilitation centre.
