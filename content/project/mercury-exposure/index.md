Mercury and other metals reach riverine birds through their aquatic prey, yet exposure varies widely among individuals and sites. Using repeated feather samples from a long-term, individual-based population of white-throated dippers in the Massif de la Chartreuse (France), I partition variation in mercury and metal(loid) concentrations among individuals, territories, rivers, and years, and test how urbanisation, agriculture, and elevation shape exposure. Related work compares blood and feather mercury across spatial scales and, using the population's social pedigree, estimates the heritability of mercury contamination.

**Collaborators:** Pierre Bize, Blandine Doligez, Gareth Norton, Lara Hinninger
