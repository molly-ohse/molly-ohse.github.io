River status and land-use indices are widely used as proxies for habitat quality, yet whether they predict the performance of vertebrates higher in the food web has rarely been tested. Using territory occupancy and breeding data from the long-term dipper population, I test how urbanisation, elevation, riparian forest, and water quality indicators relate to settlement and reproductive success, and whether dippers settle where they breed best.
